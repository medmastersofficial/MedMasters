What Is FCPS Part 1?

The Fellowship of the College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan (FCPS) Part 1 is the gateway examination for Pakistani medical graduates who want to pursue specialist training. Conducted by the College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan (CPSP), it tests your foundational knowledge of basic medical sciences before you can progress to advanced specialty training (FCPS Part 2 and beyond).

Passing FCPS Part 1 is a milestone that unlocks postgraduate residency training at CPSP-accredited institutions across Pakistan and many international centres. Because the syllabus is broad and the standard is high, structured preparation with a high-quality MCQ bank is essential.

The exam is offered in 11 specialties — Medicine & Allied, Surgery & Allied, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Diagnostic Radiology, ENT, Ophthalmology, Psychiatry, Dentistry, Anaesthesia, Pathology, and Community Medicine. Each candidate sits Paper 1 (common basic sciences) and Paper 2 (specialty-specific basic sciences).

Paper 1 — Common Basic Sciences

Paper 1 is identical across most specialties and covers: Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry, Pharmacology, Pathology, Microbiology, Community Medicine, and Behavioural Sciences. This is the foundation paper every candidate sits.

Paper 2 — Specialty-Specific Basic Sciences

Paper 2 changes based on your chosen specialty. For example, a Medicine & Allied candidate sits a Paper 2 focused on systemic medicine (CVS, GIT, CNS, etc.), while a Radiology candidate sits a Paper 2 focused on imaging principles and applied anatomy.

Who Can Apply?

FCPS Part 1 eligibility is straightforward but every requirement must be met before applying through the official CPSP portal.

  • Educational qualification: MBBS or BDS degree recognized by the Pakistan Medical & Dental Council (PMDC) or equivalent authority. BDS graduates can apply only for FCPS Part 1 in Dentistry.
  • House job / internship: Completion of a one-year house job in a PMDC-recognized institution. Where possible, at least 6 months should be in your chosen specialty (deficiencies can be compensated by equivalent residency training).
  • Registration: Valid PMDC/PMC registration certificate, or provisional registration with renewal receipt.
  • International graduates: Foreign medical degrees must be verified and equated by PMDC, with a valid home-country registration certificate.
  • Age: No upper age limit — all candidates meeting the prerequisites can apply.

Always confirm latest eligibility and application steps on the official CPSP website before applying.

How to Ace FCPS Part 1

Six high-yield strategies that consistently separate successful candidates from re-takers.

1. Understand the exam architecture first

Before you open a textbook, internalize the structure: two papers, 200 MCQs total, 75% required in each paper. Knowing that you cannot afford to skip Paper 1 basic sciences (even if your strength is in your specialty) changes how you allocate study time.

2. Cover the syllabus systematically — not randomly

Focus on the basic sciences relevant to your specialty: Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry, Pathology, Pharmacology, Microbiology, Community Medicine, and Behavioural Sciences for Paper 1, plus your chosen specialty's Paper 2 syllabus. Move system by system, not chapter by chapter at random.

3. Practice MCQs daily — not just at the end

Reading alone doesn't predict FCPS Part 1 performance; active recall does. Solve MCQs from day one, alongside your reading. Don't wait until you've "finished" a subject — you'll waste weeks. MedMasters' 30,000+ MCQ bank lets you practice subject-wise from the first day.

4. Drill recall-based past papers

The single highest-yield resource is recall-based past paper MCQs. CPSP frequently repeats questions across sittings. If you've internalised the pattern and the wording of past recalls, you walk into the hall with a head start.

5. Use a smart practice platform with tracking

A platform that tracks your accuracy per subject (and flags weak areas) saves enormous time. Instead of guessing where you're weak, you see the data. MedMasters' dashboard does this automatically — colour-coded accuracy rings, subject-wise progress, and confidence levels.

6. Take timed mock exams in the final stretch

In the last 3–4 weeks, simulate exam conditions: 100 MCQs, 2½ hours, no breaks. This trains your pace and exposes the time-management gaps that catch out under-prepared candidates.

7. Stay updated with CPSP announcements

Application dates, syllabus revisions, and procedural changes are posted on the official CPSP website. Bookmark it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sittings does CPSP hold per year?

CPSP typically conducts FCPS Part 1 examinations multiple times per year. Specific dates are announced on the official CPSP website — candidates should monitor it for current sitting schedules.

Is there negative marking in FCPS Part 1?

Negative marking policies have varied across sittings. Always check the latest CPSP guidelines before your exam date.

Can I apply for an exemption from FCPS Part 1?

Yes. Candidates holding equivalent qualifications such as MRCP, FRCS, Diplomate of the American Board, or FCPS Part 1 from Bangladesh may apply for exemption by submitting relevant documents and a processing fee to CPSP.

How long does it take to prepare for FCPS Part 1?

Most candidates prepare for 3–6 months full-time, though committed part-time preparation over 6–9 months also works. The key factor isn't time — it's MCQ-based active recall combined with focused syllabus coverage.
